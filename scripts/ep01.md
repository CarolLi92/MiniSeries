**"SHE USED TO BE MY BOSS"** | Runtime: 5:45 | English-first, selected Chinese texture

## COLD OPEN — 0:00–0:25

**[BLACK. Room tone. A kettle clicks off. Two cups on a wooden table. Morning light.]**

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

She used to be my boss.

**[CUT TO: EFFIE — digital character, early 30s, at a kitchen table, turning a mug in her hands. Through the window behind her: Toronto financial district, glass towers.]**

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

Somehow — and neither of us can name the day — she became one of the few people I feel completely comfortable telling the truth to.

**[TITLE CARD, quiet, no music swell:]**

**WHEN DOES A WORKING RELATIONSHIP BECOME A REAL FRIENDSHIP?**

## HOW WE MET — 0:25–1:05

**[B-ROLL: office floor, empty at 7:40am. A nameplate being removed years later. Quick, warm montage — hands on keyboards, hallway nods.]**

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

I reported to her for two years. She was the smartest person on the floor and the least interested in being liked.

**[CUT TO: NIA — digital character, mid-40s, in a quiet office, closing a laptop.]**

**NIA (V.O.)**

I was just trying to make the work better. I had no idea she was watching me that closely.

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

I was watching *how* she worked. Everyone else was watching *what* she delivered.

**[BEAT: NIA looks up, almost smiles — the first flicker.]**

**NIA**

She asked questions other people were too polite to ask. It was extremely inconvenient. I remember thinking: *oh. This one sees things.*

## WHEN DID WE BECOME FRIENDS? — 1:05–1:55

**[TWO-SHOT: the kitchen table. EFFIE and NIA, back to back in frame — the series' visual signature. Coffee steam.]**

**EFFIE**

Here's my version. Mentorship ended the day I stopped auditioning.

**NIA**

Auditioning?

**EFFIE**

Every meeting for two months, I was performing a "promising report." Then one day you asked me for something instead.

**NIA (V.O.)**

I asked her to teach me a tool. Half my network had already left the company. She was still there. Still answering.

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

She says I helped her. What she doesn't say: from that day, she talked to me like a colleague, not an audience.

**[PAUSE — this is the thesis line. Let it breathe.]**

## MONEY & CAREER — 1:55–2:45

**[CUT TO: coffee shop, two cups, no faces. Then NIA, direct to camera-adjacent, documentary stillness.]**

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

We don't always see things the same way. Once, she was excited about an opportunity — an idea about money being easy to make now. She wanted me in.

**[INSERT the trailer moment — the core dialogue:]**

**EFFIE**

How did you come to the conclusion that money is so easy to make now?

**[PAUSE. NIA looks away. A small smile.]**

**NIA**

That is... quite a question.

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

I said no. She was annoyed for exactly one day.

**NIA**

One *afternoon*.

**EFFIE**

And then she asked me what I thought about her plan. Not to win me back. Because my no was worth more than a yes from someone who hadn't thought about it.

**[B-ROLL: stock chart flickering, then a child's backpack by a door. Money is never abstract for us.]**

## THE ASIAN-IN-NORTH-AMERICA LAYER — 2:45–3:35

**[TWO-SHOT again. Slower. The light has moved.]**

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

Here's something we never explain at work. Where we come from, you don't question your former boss. You don't hand them a "no." You certainly don't ask how they *feel*.

**EFFIE**

It took me a few months to send her a message that began with "I disagree."

**NIA**

I still remember that message. I read it twice. Then I put the phone down and thought: *good.*

**NIA (V.O.)**

And her invitations — for a while, I read them as politeness. She'd ask, "do you want to go to this?" with the date, the venue, everything. I assumed she was being nice.

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

They were invitations. Her language just doesn't come with the word "together" attached. I had to learn to hear it.

**[B-ROLL: a ferry pulling away, two figures on opposite decks, briefly level.]**

## TRUST — 3:35–4:35

**[INTERVIEW SETUP: each woman alone, documentary framing. Alternating.]**

**EFFIE**

What can we say to each other that we can't say at work?

**NIA**

That I lost money on something. That I'm scared about a decision. That I watched a ridiculous YouTube channel for three hours and nobody can know.

**EFFIE (laughs)**

Mine is: I can tell her my accounts are bleeding and my husband earns more and I don't know if my ambition is real or borrowed —

**[BEAT. She catches herself — this one goes deep. Hold on her face.]**

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

— and she doesn't flinch, doesn't fix it, doesn't tell anyone. She just stays in the room.

**NIA (V.O.)**

Everyone performs loyalty upward. She was the one person who stayed when there was nothing to gain. You don't forget who that is. Ever.

## THE UNFINISHED US — 4:35–5:30

**[FINAL TWO-SHOT: back to back. It's evening now. The towers outside are lit.]**

**NIA**

What do I see in her?

**[PAUSE — she actually thinks.]**

**NIA**

She asks the questions no one around me asks anymore. It's extremely annoying. I would miss it immediately.

**EFFIE**

What do you see in me?

**NIA**

I see someone becoming. I don't need her to become anyone specific. I just like having a front-row seat.

**EFFIE (V.O.)**

She used to be my boss. Now she's — I still don't have a clean word for it.

**NIA**

Don't find one. Clean words are for people who've stopped changing.

## END CARD — 5:30–5:45

**[BLACK. Typewriter-quiet text:]**

*We are both still becoming who we are.*

*Maybe that's why we can see each other.*
